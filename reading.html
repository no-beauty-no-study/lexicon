The Universe

1.1 · The First Light

In an epoch so remote that no human concept of time can encompass it, the entirety of the cosmos was condensed into a singularity smaller than an atom, a point of unimaginable density wherein space and time had yet to acquire their familiar properties.

From this incomprehensible compression, the universe erupted outward with such violence that it underwent a phase of exponential inflation, expanding at a velocity that defies intuition.

Only after aeons of gradual cooling, as the primordial matter slowly dissipated its inconceivable heat, could the first beams of light finally disentangle themselves from the dense fog of particles and propagate freely across the nascent void. Early figures may only approximate the moment when this transition was complete.

That ancient radiance, now stretched and attenuated by the relentless expansion of space, still permeates the cosmos in every direction as a faint microwave background; indeed, a small fraction of the static hiss audible between the stations of an old radio is the lingering whisper of that primordial dawn.
