
Chapter 1 · The Glass Elevator

Chapter 3 · The Weight of a Scar
St. Remedy Neurological Institute terminates a cul-de-sac flanked by paulownia trees, pale stone and smoked glass that, after the hour at which visitors are discouraged, resembles less a hospital than a vessel left running after its crew has gone ashore. On the eighth floor the corridor lighting is dimmed past ten into a register that the administration considers restful and that you have come, across months of arriving at precisely this hour, to associate with the specific exhaustion of a place that never closes and never improves.
Room 812 contains a boy whose hair has outgrown the kitchen-scissor correction you administered to it last month, growing with the indifferent persistence of a body that has not been informed its occupant is elsewhere.
The visitor’s chair is occupied when the corridor light spills across the floor ahead of you.
Jael He rises with the unhurried economy of a man who has never in his life been rushed by a room he entered. “Jael Wen. Aubade Family Office. The foundation administers the research grants for the clinical-trial programme, and I am here on the sponsor’s behalf. Forgive the hour. I find the people worth meeting are rarely available during the ones the institution prefers.”
Sealyra “Sit, then. Tell me what you are selling and what it costs.”
Jael “Nothing is for sale. That is the difficulty people have with it.” The folder opens upon his knee with a deliberateness that suggests every page has been ordered to manage exactly how much a frightened person can absorb. “A flexible implant. A bridge laid across the pathways the injury severed. The brain, in the most favourable outcome, relearning the practice of attending to its own body. I will not use the word awakening, because the word does work the data has not earned.”
The cadence is calibrated. Each sentence concludes a beat before you can mount an objection to it, so that agreement arrives feeling like your own idea. You have met salesmen. You have not met one who declines to appear to be selling, and the refusal is itself the most accomplished sale you have witnessed.
Then, when the folder is closed and the gravity has been established, the register changes into something warmer, lighter, almost incidental.
Jael “It is a great deal to carry on your own. Forgive me, but I have read the intake file, and there is no second name on it. No parents listed. No other relations who visit.” A pause weighted to feel like sympathy. “Who else knows the situation is as serious as it is? Who helps you carry it?”

Chapter 4 · The Forum

Chapter 11 · Field Notes

Chapter 12 · The Seminar
The interdisciplinary seminar meets in a glass-walled room on the third floor of the social sciences building, and the convening professor, a woman who has supervised enough group projects to have abandoned optimism about them, assigns the term’s project in a single sentence and then leaves the room to its own devices.
